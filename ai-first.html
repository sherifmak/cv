what i mean when i say AI first

a blog post by Sherif Maktabi

April 2026

People keep asking me what I mean when I say "AI first." It's become one of those phrases that gets thrown around in every pitch deck and every all-hands, and I get why folks roll their eyes. So here's what I actually mean when I say it.

It's a reflex, not a strategy

AI first is the habit of reaching for a model before you reach for a spreadsheet, a teammate, or a twenty-tab research binge. It's the instinct that says "let me draft this with a model and then react to it" instead of staring at a blank page for an hour. The goal isn't to produce AI-generated work. The goal is to get to the interesting part of the problem faster.

It's not "AI only"

This is where I think a lot of teams trip. AI first doesn't mean shipping whatever the model gives you. It means the model writes the first draft, and then you, the human who actually has taste and context and accountability, do the editing. If you're skipping the editing step you're not doing AI first. You're doing AI-only, which is how you end up with slop in production.

It changes what you build

When you're AI first, you stop building features that assume a human will sit in a chair and click through a form. You start building workflows where a model does the first pass and a human reviews the tricky bits. That's a different product. It has different buttons, different defaults, different failure modes. If your roadmap still looks exactly like it did in 2022, you're not building AI first. You just bolted a chatbot onto the side of something.

It changes who you hire

The most useful person on an AI-first team is the one who can tell when a model is lying. That's a real skill, and it's not the same as being a good prompter. It's closer to being a good editor or a good code reviewer. You want people who can smell nonsense. Prompt engineering is a weekend. Taste is a career.

It's a tax on bad instincts

Here's the part nobody writes on the slide. Going AI first surfaces every sloppy process you had. If your spec was vague, the model will write something vague. If your brand voice was never documented, the model will pick one for you and it will be bland. If your code has no tests, you won't know when the model breaks something. AI first punishes fuzz. That's actually a good thing, even though it doesn't feel like one in the moment.

So what do I actually mean

When I say AI first I mean: the default first move on any task is to ask a model. The default second move is to be skeptical of what it gave you. The default third move is to ship the thing a human would be proud of. Drop any of those three and you're doing it wrong.

That's it. No framework, no maturity model, no 2×2. Just a reflex, a skepticism, and some taste.
